\section{Related Work}

Clinical-AI auditability now spans several distinct research traditions. MedLog proposes a standardized event-level logging protocol for monitoring and post-deployment analysis across single-shot and agentic clinical-AI workflows \citep{Noori.2025}. DoAtlas compiles heterogeneous evidence into executable causal estimands with explicit contrasts, uncertainty, conflict handling, and validation signals \citep{Li.2026}. HEG-TKG focuses on evidence-traceable temporal knowledge graphs and programmatically verified citations for rare-disease reasoning \citep{Ahmed.2026}. These works establish that logging, executable evidence objects, and provenance are active research areas rather than novel concepts introduced by MedScale.

Work on inference and trust promotion is an even closer prior. Evidence-Graded Decision Authorization separates information supply from inferential governance: retrieved evidence does not by itself determine what an AI system should be permitted to assert \citep{Lin.2026}. \emph{Review Before Trust} implements a deterministic source-grounded monitor between generated candidates and longitudinal-record promotion; the generator cannot certify itself, missing or ambiguous evidence fails closed, and a conformance/mutation suite tests the protected laboratory-extraction path \citep{Girda.2026}. This directly overlaps MedScale's claim that model output should not inherit source or human authority. MedScale therefore does not claim to originate generator/verifier separation or fail-closed evidence gating. The systems question studied here is broader: whether comparable authority distinctions can remain explicit across source records, deterministic derivations, models/agents, human review, external-action intents, effects, and recovery.

Evidence-ledger and audit-evidence work also narrows the provenance claim. EviLedger represents clinical-AI evidence as immutable/versioned assertions with cryptographic provenance, temporal state, and point-in-time reconstruction/rollback \citep{LiEviLedger.2026}. TrustEvidence defines a healthcare audit-evidence boundary with a FHIR R4/BALP-facing projection, exact canonical bytes, issuer authentication, adversarial mutation testing, retained verification state, and deterministic release reproduction \citep{Clim.2026}. It explicitly separates representation, signatures, backend evidence, and the claims that may be inferred from each layer. A source-verified RAG framework likewise argues for provenance and tamper-evident logging in clinical decision support, although its contribution is explicitly conceptual rather than a newly evaluated implementation \citep{Alu.2026}. CLEAR addresses a different layer: model-level adjudication across parametric knowledge, curated corpora, and retrieved evidence, with provenance/source-quality verification and additional search when conflict remains unresolved \citep{Wang.2026}. MedScale therefore does not claim novelty for cryptographic provenance, FHIR-facing audit representation, adversarial evidence testing, or exact-release reproducibility in isolation. Its provenance contribution must be supported by the joint system boundary and experiments, not by the mere presence of hashes, citations, or reproducible archives.

Operational-safety and abstention research further limits any claim of novelty around uncertainty. SA-ROC converts reliability requirements into operational zones for automation and mandatory human review \citep{Kim.2026}. Recent work specifically reviews uncertainty- and safety-driven abstention for healthcare LLMs \citep{Presacan.2026}. On-premise medical agents combine local deployment with modular agent orchestration and uncertainty-aware selective participation across diagnostic tasks \citep{Zhang.2026}. These results are important comparators for local/private inference and selective autonomy; MedScale instead evaluates how local execution interacts with system authority, provenance, external-effect boundaries, and recovery, while making no diagnostic-performance claim.

Governance frameworks emphasize that epistemic authority and responsibility must be separated from raw model capability \citep{Akgun.2026,Cao.2026}. Deterministic integrity gates have also been proposed for auditable biomedical manuscript workflows, where each stage halts on mechanically verifiable failure \citep{Nam.2026}. Monitoring reviews document the broader operational problem after deployment \citep{Andersen.2024}. FHIRTrustBench motivates interoperability quality as a contributor to trustworthiness rather than a purely infrastructural concern \citep{Bukhari.2026}.

The resulting novelty claim is deliberately narrow and falsifiable. We do not claim to originate provenance, logging, evidence gating, local inference, abstention, selective autonomy, FHIR-facing audit evidence, deterministic release reproduction, or auditable evidence objects. We test whether their systems-level complements---Core-mediated authority separation across source/derived/model/human/action/effect planes, provenance-bound artifacts, explicit subsystem failure semantics, fail-closed external boundaries, and application-state backup/restore/restart qualification---can be jointly implemented and inspected at an exact system revision. The accompanying comparison matrix uses ``not reported'' rather than ``no'' when a primary source does not establish a property.
