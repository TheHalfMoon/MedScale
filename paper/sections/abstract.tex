\begin{abstract}
Clinical and research AI systems increasingly combine heterogeneous source data, retrieval, language models, analytics, tools, and external integrations, yet system-level authority and provenance can become ambiguous as information moves across these components. We present MedScale, a local-first clinical and research AI architecture designed around explicit authority, provenance, failure semantics, and bounded effects. MedScale separates immutable source records, deterministic derivations, model-mediated artifacts, human review, action proposals, and external effects rather than treating them as interchangeable outputs. The implementation routes admitted operations through a Core authority path, represents evidence uncertainty explicitly, and applies fail-closed boundaries to network-dependent and external-effect paths. We evaluate these properties using a frozen repository snapshot and synthetic data. The evaluation program combines provenance mutation tests, adversarial failure cases, deterministic recovery experiments, cross-platform hosted-CI evidence, and performance measurements. Existing whole-platform qualification at the frozen snapshot spans 35 evidence areas and recorded two defects discovered during integrated qualification before repair and regression testing. This work does not establish clinical validity, regulatory compliance, production PHI readiness, or platform qualification; it studies whether auditability-relevant properties can be made explicit and testable as systems invariants. Quantitative paper results will be inserted only after the dedicated evaluation campaign is executed and independently reproducible from the accompanying artifact.
\end{abstract}
