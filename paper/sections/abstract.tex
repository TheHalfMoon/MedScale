\begin{abstract}
Clinical and research AI systems increasingly combine heterogeneous source data, retrieval, language models, analytics, tools, and external integrations, yet system-level authority and provenance can become ambiguous as information moves across these components. We present MedScale, a local-first clinical and research AI architecture designed around explicit authority, provenance, failure semantics, and bounded effects. MedScale separates immutable source records, deterministic derivations, model-mediated artifacts, human review, action proposals, and external effects rather than treating them as interchangeable outputs. The implementation routes admitted operations through a Core authority path, represents evidence uncertainty explicitly, and applies fail-closed boundaries to network-dependent and external-effect paths. We evaluate these properties using a frozen repository snapshot and synthetic data. At experimental revision \texttt{6a04266ae59bfb75f24d6634becd3bc4d32576c4}, 19 unique provenance/authority/effect conformance cases were executed independently on hosted Linux, macOS, and Windows runners (57 repeated platform executions total), and all completed successfully. In a preregistered follow-on at exact revision \texttt{e1cf8935f3920c95cf7229221619fbfb24fd0fa4}, the production integrated backup/restore/restart path completed successfully in three separate process-level repetitions on each of the same three operating systems (9/9 platform-repeat executions). Pre-existing whole-platform qualification at the frozen snapshot spans 35 evidence areas and recorded two defects discovered before repair and regression testing. These results are exact-revision systems evidence, not estimates of real-world attack or clinical failure probability. This work does not establish clinical validity, regulatory compliance, production PHI readiness, universal security, production disaster-recovery fitness, or platform qualification; it studies whether auditability-relevant properties can be made explicit, falsifiable, and reproducible as systems invariants.
\end{abstract}
